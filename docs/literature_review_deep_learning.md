## 1. Executive Summary

Classical paired-comparison rating systems — Bradley-Terry (1952), Elo (1978), Glicko (Glickman 1999), TrueSkill (Herbrich, Minka, Graepel 2006), Whole-History Rating (Coulom 2008), TrueSkill Through Time (Dangauthier et al. 2007) — all share a single inductive bias: each competitor is summarised by a one-dimensional latent strength, and outcomes are generated by a logistic or Thurstonian function of the strength difference. Over the last fifteen years, the academic literature has tried to relax this assumption from four directions:

1. **Multi-dimensional / vector Elo**, with explicit decomposition into transitive and cyclic components. The landmark paper is Balduzzi et al.'s "Re-evaluating Evaluation" (NeurIPS 2018, "mElo"). The mathematical foundation is combinatorial Hodge theory (Jiang, Lim, Yao, Ye 2008/2011). Recent Bayesian-Hodge work by Okahara, Nakagawa and Sugasawa (2026) shrinks the cyclic component when intransitivity is small. Hamilton, Kalenkova and Roughan (2024/2025) document that women's tennis is **about 11.5% more intransitive than men's tennis**.

2. **Neural Bradley-Terry / Plackett-Luce**, where the BT score becomes the output of a neural network. The clearest sport-relevant paper is Fujii's "Neural Bradley-Terry Rating" (ICAART 2024). The most influential LLM-side paper is Zhang et al.'s "Beyond Bradley-Terry: General Preference Model" (ICML 2025), which embeds responses in a latent space whose **skew-symmetric inner product** captures intransitivity perfectly — structurally identical to mElo for text. The matrix-factorisation precursor is Rendle et al.'s BPR (2012); the recent statistical survey is Fang et al. (arXiv:2601.14727, January 2026).

3. **Bayesian state-space refinements**. Duffield, Power and Rimella (arXiv:2308.02414, 2023) show Elo, Glicko, TrueSkill 2 and several Monte Carlo variants are all instances of one factor-graph framework; Glicko is provably a one-dimensional Extended Kalman Filter (Szczeciński and Tihoń, arXiv:2104.14012). Maystre, Kristof and Grossglauser's `kickscore` (KDD 2019) replaces the Wiener-process skill prior with a **composable Gaussian-process kernel** (constant + Wiener + Matérn + periodic + polynomial trend), still in linear time per iteration. **This is the closest "next step" from the user's TTT/WHR stack.** Microsoft's TrueSkill 2 (Minka, Cleven, Zaykov 2018) adds individual-contribution factors, squad-bias factors, mid-match-quit handling, **cross-mode skill correlation**, and biased early-career drift.

4. **Deep learning replacements and hybrids**. Maia (KDD 2020) and Maia-2 (NeurIPS 2024) are the most coherent body of work, treating the Elo rating as a *conditioning input* to a neural policy rather than as the inferred quantity. Maia-2 in particular uses a **skill-aware multi-head self-attention** mechanism with categorical Elo-bucket embeddings; this is the single most directly portable idea for tennis. McIlroy-Young et al.'s personalisation (KDD 2022) and behavioural stylometry (2022) demonstrate per-player style embeddings, with the recent Omi et al. (ICLR 2025) showing how to scale this to ~50,000 players via parameter-efficient fine-tuning. On the graph side, Clegg and Cartlidge (arXiv:2510.20454, October 2025) is the most directly relevant tennis paper: a **MagNet spectral GNN over a temporal directed match graph** that achieves 65.7% accuracy, 0.215 Brier and a **3.26% return-on-investment with Kelly staking against Pinnacle odds** on the high-intransitivity subset of ATP/WTA matches.

The 2023-2026 frontier has been dominated by **LLM evaluation and RLHF**. LMSYS / Chatbot Arena (Chiang et al., ICML 2024) formalised the Bradley-Terry MLE with bootstrap confidence intervals as the leaderboard standard. Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) showed that aligning a large language model is essentially fitting a BT likelihood with a 70B-parameter "rating function". A subsequent wave — IPO (Azar et al. 2024), KTO (Ethayarajh et al. 2024), Nash Learning from Human Feedback (Munos et al. 2023), Self-Play Preference Optimization (Wu et al. 2024), General Preference Models (Zhang et al. 2025) — has progressively relaxed the BT assumption, most notably to allow intransitive preferences. The **am-ELO** framework (Liu et al., ICML 2025) jointly estimates annotator ability and model scores, an idea that translates to weighting tennis tournaments or data sources of varying reliability.

**Key honest finding**: there is, as far as the agents could establish, **no published system that combines a TrueSkill-style Bayesian factor graph with a Bayesian neural-network observation model for sport-rating data**. Neural extensions of skill rating are either (a) point-estimate feature extractors without proper uncertainty (QuickSkill, Action2Score, GElo, OptMatch, the CNN-LSTM Lichess rater) or (b) Bayesian without being neural (kickscore, Ingram's Gaussian-process priors, hierarchical Stan models). This is the most defensible "open territory" for a tennis researcher.

## 2. The Classical Foundations (and Why They Matter)

A useful way to read everything that follows is through the lens of the **unification papers**:

- **Szczeciński and Tihoń** (*JQAS* 19(4):295-315, 2023; arXiv:2104.14012) [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] show that Elo, Glicko and TrueSkill are instances of a single approximate Kalman-filter family. **Glicko is a one-dimensional Extended Kalman Filter.**
- **Duffield, Power, Rimella** (arXiv:2308.02414, 2023) [READ PARTIAL] generalise this to a state-space factor-graph framework that admits Elo, Glicko, TrueSkill 2, sequential Monte Carlo and finite-state HMMs as modular instances. Code: https://github.com/SamDuffield/abile. No neural components are included in their framework, **but neural emissions or transitions plug in cleanly** — this is one of the most natural research entry points.

The historic family is well summarised by Firth et al.'s "Many routes to the ubiquitous Bradley-Terry model" (arXiv:2312.13619, 2023). For dates and lineage, the key entries are:

| System | Origin | Inference | Key property |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bradley-Terry | Bradley & Terry, *Biometrika* 1952 | MLE | Scalar strength, logistic link |
| Elo | Elo 1978 (book) | Online SGD on logistic loss | Tunable K, no explicit uncertainty |
| Glicko / Glicko-2 | Glickman, *Applied Statistics* 48, 1999; 2001 | Online filtering, EKF in disguise | Adds rating deviation (RD) |
| Stephenson | Kaggle FIDE 2010-2012 | Glicko-Boost extension | Won the 2010 Kaggle chess challenge |
| TrueSkill | Herbrich, Minka, Graepel, NeurIPS 2006 | EP on factor graph | Gaussian skill, team additivity, draw margin |
| WHR | Coulom, CG 2008 | Batch Newton-Raphson on tridiagonal Hessian | Wiener-process prior on skill curve |
| TTT | Dangauthier, Herbrich, Minka, Graepel, NeurIPS 2007 | EP over time chain | TrueSkill + smoothing over years |
| TrueSkill 2 | Minka, Cleven, Zaykov, MSR-TR-2018-8 | Extended EP | Individual stats, squads, quits, cross-mode |
| Kickscore | Maystre, Kristof, Grossglauser, KDD 2019 | Coordinate-ascent EP, linear time | GP prior with composable kernels |
| OpenSkill | Joshy, JOSS 2024 | Weng-Lin closed-form Plackett-Luce | Patent-free TrueSkill alternative |

The mathematical lineage is summarised in `rating_systems/README.md` and aligns with what the agents found. None of the **classical** entries is neural; everything that is "neural" sits on top of or alongside this skeleton.

A separate older but recently revived line is **combinatorial Hodge theory** for ranking (Jiang, Lim, Yao, Ye, *Math. Program.* 2011) [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY], which decomposes the comparison-graph edge flow into a gradient (transitive) component, a harmonic (globally cyclic) component, and a curl (locally cyclic) component. This decomposition is the mathematical backbone of all later "vector Elo" work.

## 3. The mElo / Vector-Elo Line

### 3.1 Balduzzi et al. (NeurIPS 2018) — "Re-evaluating Evaluation"

arXiv:1806.02643. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Each agent has a scalar Elo `r_i` and a `k`-dimensional vector `c_i`. The match-outcome logit is approximated by

$$\text{logit}(P_{ij}) \approx r_i - r_j + c_i^{\mathsf T} \Omega\, c_j,$$

where `Ω` is a *skew-symmetric* matrix (so the dot-product term flips sign when `i` and `j` swap, preserving the BT antisymmetry). Skew-symmetric matrices have even rank `2k`; the model is called `mElo2k`. Updates are SGD on the logistic loss with `Ω`-antisymmetry preserved by parameterisation. The accompanying contribution is **Nash averaging** for multi-task evaluation.

The design is motivated by three properties: invariance to redundant agents/tasks (P1), continuity (P2), and interpretability (P3). Empirically demonstrated on Hearthstone, AlphaStar internals, 10,5-Blotto, and a Go tournament between seven AlphaGo variants plus Zen.

Code: an R port at https://github.com/dclaz/mELO; documentation at https://dclaz.github.io/mELO/.

**Critique for tennis use**. mElo was designed for *game-theoretic* intransitivity (rock-paper-scissors patterns in policy space), not for *contextual* intransitivity (surface specialists, head-to-head cycles). The model has no time dynamics and no surface awareness; the cyclic dimensions are hard to identify without millions of matches. Hamilton et al. (2024, see below) suggest the cyclic component in tennis is around 11.5% larger for WTA than ATP — real but not dominant.

### 3.2 α-Rank, Matrix Completion and Related

- **Omidshafiei et al. (2019)** "α-Rank: Multi-Agent Evaluation by Evolution", *Scientific Reports*, arXiv:1903.01373. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Markov-Conley chains replace Nash equilibrium as the evaluation target; polynomial-time, sub-cubic in agent count.
- **Du, Yan, Chen, Wang, Zhang (2021)** "Estimating α-Rank from a Few Entries with Low Rank Matrix Completion", ICML 2021, PMLR 139:2870-2879. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Nuclear-norm minimisation on the antisymmetric payoff matrix.

### 3.3 Hodge-Theoretic Bayesian Treatments

- **Strang, Abbott, Thomas (2021)** "The Network HHD", *SIAM Review*, arXiv:2011.01825. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Rigorous Helmholtz-Hodge Decomposition for tournament networks.
- **Hamilton, Kalenkova, Roughan (2024)** "Elo Ratings in the Presence of Intransitivity", arXiv:2412.14427 (also PLOS ONE 2025). [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Shows Elo's converged ratings depend on the *matchup schedule* when intransitivity is non-zero; quantifies WTA-ATP intransitivity gap (~11.5%).
- **Okahara, Nakagawa, Sugasawa (2026)** "The Bayesian Intransitive Bradley-Terry Model via Combinatorial Hodge Theory", arXiv:2601.07158. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Each pair gets a gradient (transitive) and curl (cycle) component with **global-local shrinkage priors on the curl**, collapsing to classical BT when intransitivity is absent. Gibbs sampler. **The most recent principled Bayesian treatment.**

### 3.4 The LLM-Side Rediscovery: General Preference Models

**Zhang, Zhang, Wu, Xu, Gu (ICML 2025)** "Beyond Bradley-Terry Models: A General Preference Model for Language Model Alignment", arXiv:2410.02197 [ABSTRACT]; code https://github.com/general-preference/general-preference-model. Each LLM response is embedded as a vector `v_i`; preference probability is `σ(v_i^{\mathsf T} A v_j)` with `A` learned and **skew-symmetric**. This is structurally **identical to mElo** with `k≥1`, except the embeddings are learned end-to-end from text rather than from outcomes. Reports near-perfect accuracy on synthetic cyclic preferences where vanilla BT performs at chance; outperforms BT on RewardBench by up to 5.6 points.

**The clearest LLM-to-tennis transfer is here.** The user could literally instantiate "GPM-for-tennis": player embedding `v_i` (from match-history features) dotted through skew-symmetric `A` with `v_j`. This is mElo for tennis, with the cyclic structure learned rather than spectrally decomposed.

## 4. Neural Bradley-Terry and Related Parameterisations

### 4.1 Foundational and Classical
- **Causeur and Husson (2005)** "A 2-dimensional extension of the Bradley-Terry model", *J. Statist. Plann. Inference* 135(2):245-259. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] First explicit two-dimensional BT in the statistics literature. Each item has two scores; MDS inside a logistic model; MLE + asymptotic confidence ellipses. Applied to cornflakes consumer preference.
- **Rendle et al. (2009, 2012)** "BPR: Bayesian Personalized Ranking from Implicit Feedback", arXiv:1205.2618. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] The canonical implicit-feedback pairwise comparison objective: `σ(score(u, i_pos) - score(u, i_neg))` over latent factor vectors. **Mathematically a multidimensional BT** where the latent embedding is a factor vector and the score is a dot product.
- **Menke and Martinez (2008)** "A Bradley-Terry artificial neural network model for individual ratings in group competitions", *Neural Computing and Applications*. [SECONDARY] Re-parameterises BT as a one-layer ANN inside a state-space view of skills as latent random walks; predates the deep-learning wave.

### 4.2 Neural Bradley-Terry Rating (Fujii, ICAART 2024)

arXiv:2307.13709. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Item features `f(x)` are mapped through a shared-weight network `φ` to scalar scores, then combined via the BT functional `σ(φ(f(x_i)) - φ(f(x_j)))`. Trained end-to-end on pairwise outcomes. Optional "advantage adjuster" module handles asymmetric comparisons. **Key feature**: generalises to unseen items via their features.

The structural pattern is the simplest possible deep BT and is the natural building block for a "neural Glicko" or "neural Elo". The original paper does not include time dynamics.

### 4.3 Multidimensional BT with MCMC

"Individual Differences Multidimensional BT Using Reversible Jump MCMC", *Behaviormetrika* 37(2). [SECONDARY] RJMCMC over the embedding dimensionality. Of mostly methodological interest; not high-profile.

### 4.4 Bradley-Terry Stochastic Block Model (2025)

arXiv:2511.03467. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Embeds BT inside a stochastic block model so items cluster into tiers; applied to ATP 2000-2022 men's data and finds that the top 100 partition into 3-4 tiers. Gibbs sampler. A nice mid-point between "single rating per player" and "vector embedding per player" and the rare BT extension that has been explicitly fitted to ATP data.

### 4.5 Reward Modelling Beyond BT in RLHF

- **Sun, Shen, Ton (ICLR 2025)** "Rethinking Bradley-Terry Models in Preference-Based Reward Modeling", arXiv:2411.04991 [ABSTRACT]; code https://github.com/holarissun/RewardModelingBeyondBradleyTerry. Argues only *order consistency* matters: any monotone transform of the BT score works, so a simpler upper-bound classifier suffices. 12,000+ experimental setups.
- The "Recent advances in Bradley-Terry" survey (**Fang, Han, Luo, Xu, arXiv:2601.14727, January 2026**) [ABSTRACT] is the single best entry point into the modern BT literature, explicitly bridging classical statistics with LLM-RLHF.

### 4.6 Generalised BT with Covariates

- arXiv:2507.22472 "Inference in a generalised BT model with covariates and a growing number of subjects". [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] The logit becomes `β_i - β_j + Z^{\mathsf T} γ`; classical statistics treatment, asymptotic normality of MLE.
- arXiv:2503.18256 covariate-shift inference. [SECONDARY]

This is mathematically the cleanest formal underpinning for the user's existing covariate-aware systems.

## 5. Bayesian Time-Varying Extensions (the user's home turf)

### 5.1 The Maystre line: `kickscore`

**Maystre, Kristof, Grossglauser (KDD 2019)** "Pairwise Comparisons with Flexible Time-Dynamics", arXiv:1903.07746. [READ FULL by the TrueSkill agent — the most fully read paper in the review.] Code: https://github.com/lucasmaystre/kickscore (MIT, Python, on PyPI and conda).

Each player has a latent score function `s_i(t)` with a **Gaussian-process prior**. The observation model is Bernoulli with logistic link on `s_i(t) - s_j(t)`. Supported kernels are composable by addition:

- Constant (recovers static Bradley-Terry)
- Wiener / Brownian motion (recovers TTT / WHR)
- Matérn (1/2, 3/2, 5/2)
- Exponential / Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
- Periodic (season cycles)
- Polynomial trend (long-term drift)

Inference is coordinate-ascent EP. The crucial trick is that all supported kernels have a **Markovian state-space representation**, so each player-history sweep runs in linear time via closed-form Kalman-filter / RTS-smoother messages. Tested on NBA, ATP, association football and chess datasets and reported to outperform TrueSkill, Elo, and decayed Elo on out-of-sample log-loss, especially where the underlying skill has career-arc or seasonal structure.

**Kickscore is the natural next step from the user's WHR / TTT stack.** A direct extension — a **multi-output kickscore with covariate kernels** of the form `s_i(t, x) = s_i^{base}(t) + h_{surface}(t) + β^{\mathsf T} x` — is mathematically straightforward and, as far as the agents found, not yet published.

Related Maystre work: "Fast and Accurate Inference of Plackett-Luce Models" (NeurIPS 2015) [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] gives the linear-time spectral inference that underpins OpenSkill; "Just Sort It!" (ICML 2017, arXiv:1502.05556) [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] is active-comparison theory; Maystre's EPFL PhD thesis (2018, Distinction award) collects the framework.

### 5.2 Gaussian-Process Priors for Dynamic Paired Comparison (Ingram 2019)

arXiv:1902.07378. [READ PARTIAL] Replaces the Wiener-process prior of WHR/TTT with a generic GP prior (the implementation uses Matérn-style kernels via GPy). Sparse-linear-algebra Laplace approximation. Bayesian-optimisation hyperparameter tuning. Empirics on the 2018 ATP season: beats Elo and Glicko on log-loss, especially with surface covariates. Code: https://github.com/martiningram/paired-comparison-gp-laplace.

Together with Maystre's kickscore, these two are **the leading GP-prior dynamic-rating papers** and the direct intellectual peers of the user's TTT.

### 5.3 Ingram's Point-Based Bayesian Hierarchical Model (JQAS 2019)

Ingram, M. *Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports* 15(4):313-325, 2019. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY, PDF unparseable in agent session] Each player has a latent **serve skill** and **return skill**, each evolving as a Gaussian random walk through time, with surface and tournament effects. Hierarchical point-win probability. MCMC fit. Reported 68.8% accuracy and 0.592 log-loss on the 2014 ATP season (vs 66.3% / 0.641 for the best comparator at the time).

This is the **cleanest published tennis-specific Bayesian model to neuralise**: replace the linear surface/time effects with a neural function of `(player_id_embedding, opponent_id_embedding, surface, time, score_context)` while keeping the serve/return Bernoulli structure as the outer probabilistic model.

### 5.4 TrueSkill 2 (Microsoft Research 2018)

Minka, T., Cleven, R., Zaykov, Y. Microsoft Research Technical Report MSR-TR-2018-8, 22 March 2018. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/trueskill-2-improved-bayesian-skill-rating-system/. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY for all agents — the MSR PDF was not retrievable.]

Six extensions over TrueSkill:

1. **Individual-contribution factors.** Observed in-game statistics (kills, deaths, assists, score) attach as leaf factors to the player's match-performance node. Each statistic has its own observation noise and scale. **The single most important change for modern shooter matchmaking.**
2. **Squad / party effect.** Co-queued subsets get a positive bias factor.
3. **Quit modelling.** Mid-match quit treated as conditional surrender.
4. **Cross-mode skill correlation.** A Gaussian factor with correlation `ρ_{AB}` couples skills in modes A and B. New-mode priors are informed by other-mode posteriors. **Direct analogue of cross-surface skill correlation in tennis.**
5. **Biased early-career drift.** `s_{t+1} ~ N(s_t + γ_t, τ^2)` with positive drift `γ_t` decaying with games played.
6. **Batch / online inference.** EP on the extended factor graph, runnable either as filtering (online) or smoothing (batch).

Empirical: on Halo 5 data, **68% match-outcome prediction accuracy vs 52% for original TrueSkill** — a very large delta. Integrated into Halo 5 ranked playlists in May 2018 and used in Gears of War 4. No official Microsoft code release; `SkylakeXx/TrueSkill2` on GitHub is a third-party paper implementation. The Erlang `mmooyyii/trueskill` library advertises both classic and TrueSkill 2.

**Two ideas transfer cleanly to tennis**: (a) per-match individual-stat factors (serve-points-won, return-points-won, break-points-saved) attached to the player's match-performance node, and (b) **cross-surface skill correlation** — the direct analogue of cross-mode correlation. Squad and quit machinery does not transfer.

### 5.5 OpenSkill (Joshy, JOSS 2024)

arXiv:2401.05451. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Open-source Python library implementing the Weng-Lin (2011) approximations to TrueSkill plus a Plackett-Luce variant (Guiver-Snelson 2009) extended with variance parameters. Five models: BradleyTerryFull/Part, ThurstoneMostellerFull/Part, recommended PlackettLuce. **Sidesteps the TrueSkill US patent (valid until April 2029)** via simpler Plackett-Luce updates. Reports ≥3× speedup over the canonical `sublee/trueskill` Python library with comparable accuracy. https://openskill.me/.

### 5.6 Deep / Generative Plackett-Luce

- **DATELINE** (Hu et al., arXiv:1812.05877, 2018) [SECONDARY] — Deep Plackett-Luce with uncertainty for crowdsourcing. Closest existing "deep PL + uncertainty" model.
- **Schäfer and Hüllermeier** (2017/2018) — dyad ranking with PL on joint embeddings. [SECONDARY]
- **Ma et al.** (arXiv:2006.05067, 2020) — PL with partitioned preference for fast neural learning-to-rank. [SECONDARY]

## 6. Time-Varying Neural Models

### 6.1 RNN-Based Skill Estimation
- **QuickSkill** (Zhang et al., CIKM 2022, arXiv:2208.07704) [ABSTRACT] — sequence-based deep network (LSTM-style) takes the first few per-game performance vectors of a new player and predicts the future converged TrueSkill value. **The killer cold-start application.** Uncertainty is implicit (point estimate); not a Bayesian extension. Code not public.
- **Octosport.io blog** [SECONDARY] — practitioner framing of Elo as a recurrent network with rating-as-hidden-state and BT-sigmoid prediction head; tutorial-only.

### 6.2 Move-Level / Per-Game Rating Predictors

- **Omori and Tadepalli (CG 2024)** "Chess Rating Estimation from Moves and Clock Times Using a CNN-LSTM", arXiv:2409.11506. [READ PARTIAL] Per-position CNN extracts board features; concatenated with clock-time scalars; bidirectional LSTM emits per-ply Elo estimates. MAE 182 Elo on a >1M-game Lichess test set. **Inherits the noise of Lichess ratings** because trained as supervised regression, but works on a single game (essentially zero-history cold start). **Near-direct analogue of what could be built for tennis** if shot-level data were available.
- **GlickFormer (Miłosz et al., arXiv:2410.11078, 2024)** [SECONDARY] — ChessFormer + factorised spatio-temporal transformer predicting the Glicko-2 difficulty of a chess puzzle (4.16M puzzles). Position-difficulty, not player-skill, but a useful example of using Glicko-2 as a transformer regression target.

### 6.3 Maia, Maia-2 and Maia-Style Personalisation

- **Maia (McIlroy-Young, Sen, Kleinberg, Anderson, KDD 2020)**, arXiv:2006.01855. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY; the Toronto-hosted PDF was denied] Customised AlphaZero-style residual ConvNet supervised to predict the human move, not the optimal move. Nine separate models for Lichess rating buckets 1100-1900 in 100-point increments. Maia-X peaks accuracy on games by humans rated near X — **"tunable Elo" behaviourally**. Code: https://github.com/CSSLab/maia-chess. Lichess bots maia1 / maia5 / maia9 have played over two million games.

- **Maia-2 (Tang, Jiao, McIlroy-Young, Kleinberg, Sen, Anderson, NeurIPS 2024)**, arXiv:2409.20553. [READ PARTIAL] **A unified network with categorical skill-level embeddings injected via skill-aware multi-head self-attention**:

  $$Q^*_k = Q_k + (e_a \oplus e_o) W^*$$

  where `e_a` and `e_o` are the active and opponent skill-bucket embeddings. The network adaptively weights position features based on the skill levels of *both* players. 12-block ResNet board encoder + transformer; three heads (policy, value, auxiliary). Trained on 169M Lichess rapid games (9.1B positions, Jan 2013 – Nov 2023). **+1.9 percentage points move-prediction accuracy over Maia-1 (53.25% vs 51.32%)**, perplexity 4.07 bits, 27% of test positions show monotonic skill-conditioned move correctness. Code: https://github.com/CSSLab/maia2.

  **This is the single most directly portable idea in the entire review.** A tennis analogue feeds surface-specific Elo embeddings for the server and returner into an attention layer over point context. The existing tennis_ratings system already maintains the per-player Elo states required.

- **Personalised Maia (McIlroy-Young et al., KDD 2022, arXiv:2008.10086)** [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] — per-player fine-tuning. Needs ~5000 games to beat the base model.
- **Behavioural Stylometry (McIlroy-Young et al., arXiv:2208.01366, 2022)** [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] — transformer-based few-shot classifier identifies the specific player from a small set of games. 98% accuracy on 1-of-thousands with 100 labelled games.
- **Generative Stylometry at Scale (Omi, Caccia, Sarkar, Ash, Sen, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2502.14998)** [READ FULL via abstract] — per-player modelling as multi-task learning with **parameter-efficient fine-tuning**. Each of 47,864 chess players (and 2,000 Rocket League players, 10,177 celebrities for image generation) gets an explicit **style vector** that selectively activates shared parameters. Style vectors are generative and steerable. **The most modern formulation of player personalisation**; an analogue for the ATP/WTA top 500 is straightforward.

- **"Maia4All" / "Learning to Imitate with Less" (arXiv:2507.21488, 2025)** [SECONDARY] reportedly reduces the personalisation game count from ~5000 to ~20.

### 6.4 Graph Neural Networks

- **Wang (2023) "Graph Embedding Augmented Skill Rating" (GElo)**, arXiv:2304.08257. [READ FULL] Skill-gap graph; DeepWalk/node2vec embeddings; cosine-similarity + activeness terms adjust Elo updates. Cold-start helped via embedding similarity. **Embeddings are offline / batch — not a true online rating system.**
- **Clegg and Cartlidge (2025) "Intransitive Player Dominance and Market Inefficiency in Tennis Forecasting"**, arXiv:2510.20454. [READ PARTIAL — the most-quoted paper of this review] **MagNet** spectral GNN for directed graphs using the magnetic Laplacian (complex Hermitian, tunable `q=0.25`); Chebyshev polynomial order `K=2`, two layers, 4-hop receptive field. Three surface-specific directed graphs per gender (clay/grass/hard) with edges weighted by

  $$D = \frac{\sum \alpha\,\beta\,\phi\,g}{\sum \alpha\,\beta\,\phi}$$

  where `α` is surface transferability, `β` is tournament prestige, `φ` is exponential time decay (learnable `λ=0.38`), and `g` encodes the match result. Static node features: height, weight, birth date, handedness. Dynamic node features: surface-specific in/out-degree.

  Reported empirics:

  | Method | Accuracy | Brier |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | Pinnacle bookmaker odds | 69.0% | 0.196 |
  | Weighted Elo | 66.5% | 0.212 |
  | Standard Elo | 65.8% | 0.215 |
  | MagNet GNN | 65.7% | 0.215 |

  On the high-intransitivity subset, the GNN reports **3.26% ROI with Kelly staking** over 1,903 bets against Pinnacle. The full-sample GNN is competitive with Elo but trails bookmaker markets; the GNN's value is on the intransitive subset.

- **Knowledge-Enhanced Graph Contrastive Learning (KEGC)** [SECONDARY] — match-sequence graphs with edges connecting similar matches for skill-evolution capture.
- **"Who You Play Affects How You Play" (GATv2-TCN, arXiv:2303.16741)** [SECONDARY] — Graph Attention Network v2 + temporal convolutions over a dynamic player-interaction graph for performance prediction.

### 6.5 Neural Kalman / Neural EP / Neural Hawkes (the missing pieces)

- **KalmanNet (Revach et al., 2022)**, arXiv:2107.10043, *IEEE Trans. Signal Processing*. [READ ABSTRACT] Replaces the analytic covariance recursions of the Kalman update with a GRU. **No published application to player skill** — a clear open gap given that the user's classical systems are all approximate Kalman filters.
- **Heess, Tarlow, Winn (NeurIPS 2013)** "Learning to Pass Expectation Propagation Messages". [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Trains neural networks to emit EP messages. The conceptual ingredient for a "neural-EP TrueSkill" but **not yet applied to skill rating**.
- **Akbayrak, Bocharov, de Vries (2021)** "Variational Message Passing and Local Constraint Manipulation in Factor Graphs", *Entropy* 23(7):807. [SECONDARY] Theory-level enabler for hybrid models.
- **"Learning in Deep Factor Graphs with Gaussian Belief Propagation" (arXiv:2311.14649)** [SECONDARY] — adjacent theoretical work, not skill rating.
- **Neural Hawkes / Transformer Hawkes processes** (Mei and Eisner, NeurIPS 2017; Zuo et al., ICML 2020) [SECONDARY] — continuous-time event sequences with neural emissions. **No paper located applies them to player rating**, despite matches being precisely an irregular event stream with intensity tied to activity and form.

## 7. Tennis-Specific Work: A Self-Contained Section

Because this is the user's primary application domain, the tennis literature is pulled together here in one place.

### 7.1 Foundational Statistical Models
- **Klaassen and Magnus (2001)** "Are Points in Tennis IID?", *JASA* 96(454):500-509. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] Demonstrates that tennis points are *neither* independent nor identically distributed (serving on important points is harder; prior-point outcome carries information). Yet IID is a useful first approximation.

- **Sackmann (2019)** "An Introduction to Tennis Elo" on Tennis Abstract / Heavy Topspin. [SECONDARY, agent fetch returned 403] Maintains four parallel Elos (overall + per-surface) and reports a 50/50 surface blend, with the weighting determined empirically.
- **Kovalchik (2016)** "Searching for the GOAT of tennis win prediction", *JQAS* 12(3):127-138. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] **Finds 538's surface-blended Elo to be the best of the public methods at beating bookmaker prices.**
- **Kovalchik (2020)** "Extension of the Elo rating system to margin of victory", *IJF* 36(4):1329-1341. [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] The K factor becomes a function of dominance (sets, games, break-points).
- **Kovalchik and Albert (2022)** arXiv:2202.00583 "A Statistical Model of Serve Return Impact Patterns in Professional Tennis". [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY]

### 7.3 Bayesian Hierarchical Tennis
- **Ingram (2019)** point-based Bayesian hierarchical model, *JQAS* 15(4) — see §5.3. The cleanest published peer of TTT applied to tennis.
- **Ingram (2019)** GP-prior dynamic paired-comparison, arXiv:1902.07378 — see §5.2.

### 7.4 Recent Deep-Learning / Graph Tennis (2023-2026)
- **Buhamra and Groll (2025)** "Statistical Enhanced Learning for Modeling and Prediction Tennis Matches at Grand Slam Tournaments", arXiv:2502.01613. [READ FULL via abstract] Treats Elo as a feature inside random forests / regression, not as the final score. Modest improvement; mostly confirms that Elo encodes information XGBoost cannot recover from raw features alone.

- **EOMM (Chen et al., WWW 2017, arXiv:1702.06820)** [ABSTRACT/SECONDARY] — EA's engagement-optimised matchmaking, treats matchmaking as churn optimisation rather than skill balancing.

## 9. The LLM Frontier (2023-2026)

### 9.1 Bradley-Terry as the LLM Leaderboard Standard
- **Zheng, Chiang et al. (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.05685)** "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" [ABSTRACT] — introduced the crowdsourced battle platform plus MT-Bench (80 expert-curated questions). GPT-4-as-judge ≥80% agreement with humans. Documents position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases.
- **Chiang et al. (ICML 2024, arXiv:2403.04132)** "Chatbot Arena" [ABSTRACT] — formalised the move from streaming Elo to **static Bradley-Terry MLE on the full vote graph** with bootstrap confidence intervals, by March 2024 over 240K votes on 50+ models.
- **LMSYS Style Control (Frick et al., Aug 2024 blog)** [SECONDARY] — extended the BT regression with **style features as independent confounders** (response length, markdown headers, list count). The "Style-Controlled Arena" leaderboard.
- **Arena-Hard / BenchBuilder (Li, Chiang, Frick, arXiv:2406.11939, 2024)** [ABSTRACT] — 500 difficult auto-curated prompts, judged by GPT-4.1 / Gemini-2.5, 98.6% correlation with human Arena preferences at ~$20 cost.
- **Auto-Arena (Zhuo et al., arXiv:2405.20267, 2024)** [SECONDARY] — peer-battles between LLMs with LLM-jury voting, 92.14% correlation with humans.

### 9.2 Critiques and Annotator-Aware Ratings
- **Zhao, Rush, Goyal (arXiv:2412.04363, Dec 2024)** [ABSTRACT] — 10% poor-quality votes can shift the top of the Arena leaderboard by up to 5 places. Demonstrates a vote-rigging attack.
- **Vyas et al. (arXiv:2508.11847, 2025)** [SECONDARY] — top-end BT/Elo rankings are remarkably sensitive to dropping small numbers of pairwise votes.
- **Min et al. (arXiv:2501.17858, 2025)** [SECONDARY] — active vote-rigging attack on BT-based Arena scores.
- **am-ELO (Liu et al., ICML 2025, arXiv:2505.03475)** [ABSTRACT]. **Two contributions**: m-ELO replaces iterative Elo with MLE for theoretical consistency; **am-ELO modifies the BT/Elo probability function so that annotator ability is jointly estimated with model scores**. Essentially item-response theory for raters. **This translates straightforwardly to weighting tennis tournaments or data sources of varying reliability.**
- **Boubdir et al. (arXiv:2406.07791, 2024)** "Judging the Judges: Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" [SECONDARY] — position bias varies sharply across judges and tasks.
- **Li et al. (arXiv:2411.15594, 2024-2025)** "A Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge" [SECONDARY].

### 9.3 RLHF as Bradley-Terry Optimisation
- **Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.18290)** [ABSTRACT] — **assumes BT preferences**, re-parameterises the optimal RLHF reward via a closed-form mapping to policy log-ratios; policy training becomes binary cross-entropy on (chosen, rejected) pairs. Implicit reward `r(x, y) = β log π(y|x)/π_ref(y|x) + const`. No PPO loop. Matches PPO-RLHF on summarisation and sentiment. **Conceptually, the BT log-likelihood now trains 70B-parameter neural raters.**
- **IPO / ΨPO (Azar et al., AISTATS 2024, arXiv:2310.12036)** [ABSTRACT] — generic preference-learning objective; drops the BT logistic link in IPO, training so the log-probability gap hits a fixed margin. More robust when preferences are deterministic.
- **KTO (Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.01306)** [ABSTRACT] — Kahneman-Tversky prospect-theoretic utility; **needs only binary thumbs-up/thumbs-down, not paired comparisons**. The most radical departure from BT.
- **Nash Learning from Human Feedback (Munos et al., arXiv:2312.00886, 2023)** [ABSTRACT] — recasts alignment as a **symmetric two-player preference game** with Nash equilibrium as solution; explicit motivation is that BT assumes transitivity but real preferences are intransitive.
- **Self-Play Preference Optimization (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.00675)** [ABSTRACT] — operationalises NLHF as a constant-sum two-player game with iterative policy updates approximating Nash. Bypasses BT entirely. Mistral-7B with PairRM oracle reaches 28.53% length-controlled win-rate vs GPT-4-Turbo on AlpacaEval 2.0.
- **General Preference Model / GPM (Zhang et al., ICML 2025, arXiv:2410.02197)** — see §3.4. **Structurally mElo for text.**
- **Rethinking BT in Preference-Based Reward Modeling (Sun, Shen, Ton, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2411.04991)** [ABSTRACT] — only *order consistency* matters; any monotone transform of the BT score works.
- **PURM (arXiv:2503.22480, 2025)** and **BNRM (arXiv:2602.10623, 2026)** [SECONDARY] — generalise BT to learn a *distribution* over rewards. **Bayesian-deep-learning analogue of Glicko's RD.**
- **RewardBench / RewardBench 2 (Lambert, Pyatkin, Morrison, arXiv:2403.13787, 2024; arXiv:2506.01937, 2025)** [ABSTRACT]. Standard benchmarks for reward models.

### 9.4 Recent Surveys (must-reads)
- **Fang, Han, Luo, Xu (arXiv:2601.14727, January 2026)** "Recent advances in the Bradley-Terry Model" [ABSTRACT] — **the single best up-to-date statistical survey**, explicitly bridging classical BT with LLM RLHF.
- **Zhong, Shen, Li et al. (arXiv:2504.12328, 2025)** "A Comprehensive Survey of Reward Models" [ABSTRACT].
- **Kaufmann, Weng, Bengs, Hüllermeier (arXiv:2312.14925, 2023; TMLR 2025)** "A Survey of RLHF" [ABSTRACT].
- **Xiao, Wang, Gan et al. (arXiv:2410.15595, 2024-2025)** "Comprehensive Survey of DPO" [ABSTRACT].

## 11. Gaps and Frontier: The Tennis Researcher's Opportunities

Consolidating across all five agents' findings, the gaps that look genuinely under-explored and that match the user's existing stack are:

### A. Vector-valued time-varying ratings (the natural extension of WHR/TTT)
1. **Multi-output `kickscore` with covariate kernels.** Maystre's framework already supports GP-prior skill curves in linear time. A direct extension `s_i(t, x) = s_i^{base}(t) + h_{surface}(t) + β^{\mathsf T} x` is mathematically straightforward but not, as far as we can tell, published.
2. **Cross-surface skill correlation via factor-graph EP.** TrueSkill 2's cross-mode correlation translates directly to tennis surface correlation. A clean Bayesian factor-graph implementation has not appeared.
3. **A vector-valued GP per player with kernel structure capturing surface-correlation.** Maystre's kernels are scalar, Ingram's GP-prior model is scalar; a multi-output GP with structured between-surface covariance is open territory.

### B. Bayesian-neural hybrids for skill rating
4. **TrueSkill 2 with the team-likelihood factor replaced by a Bayesian neural network**, trained variationally on per-player features. Neural-EP message learners (Heess et al. 2013), variational message passing on factor graphs (Akbayrak et al. 2021), and deep Plackett-Luce (DATELINE) are all available ingredients; no one appears to have combined them for sport rating.
5. **End-to-end neural Bradley-Terry with WHR-style smoothness regularisation on each player's embedding trajectory.** GPM/NBTR assume i.i.d. comparisons; sports outcomes are massively non-stationary.
6. **KalmanNet-style learned-gain dynamics applied to player skill.** Glicko is provably an EKF; replacing the analytic covariance recursion with a small GRU is a clear research thread.

### C. Maia-style ideas, ported to tennis
7. **A Maia-2 analogue for tennis.** Take a transformer over rally tokens or score-context features, condition on per-surface Elo embeddings via skill-aware attention. The existing tennis_ratings system supplies the conditioning vectors. **The single most directly portable idea in the literature.**
8. **Per-player style vectors via parameter-efficient fine-tuning** (Omi et al. 2025). 8-16D vectors per ATP/WTA top-500 player; the style vectors become covariates in the rating update — a learned, data-driven handicap.
9. **Behavioural-stylometry tennis embeddings from MCP shot-by-shot data.** Identify the player from rally patterns; the embedding space gives a free per-player feature.

### D. Cold start and feature priors
10. **Neural-feature priors on Glicko / WHR initialisation.** No work places an embedding prior on a Glicko player's initial rating distribution. **Low-hanging fruit** for junior-to-senior transitions, where features (height, ITF results, country) are available.
11. **A Rating-PFN.** A TabPFN-style transformer pre-trained on synthetic WHR/TTT match streams, doing zero-shot Bayesian rating updates in-context. **Open opportunity.**

### E. Intransitivity and margin-of-victory
12. **Vector-Elo (mElo-style) versus scalar Elo, properly cross-validated on tennis.** Balduzzi et al. were not strict about CV; tennis offers a clean tournament-by-tournament temporal split. Combined with Hamilton et al.'s WTA/ATP intransitivity result, this is a publishable question.
13. **Does the cyclic component of vector Elo carry margin-of-victory information?** Combining Kovalchik's MOV Elo with mElo's cyclic component is unexplored.
14. **The user's existing weighted/covariate Glicko systems already implement what LMSYS only adopted for LLM evaluation in mid-2024.** Stylistically this is worth surfacing: tennis was ahead of the LLM curve on covariate-aware BT.

### F. Calibration and robustness
15. **Calibration-aware variational inference for rating systems.** Most neural rating papers drop the Bayesian posterior; classical methods give one but rarely audit it. The CS:GO paper (Bober-Irizar et al. 2024) is one of the few exercises in calibration auditing.
16. **am-ELO-style joint annotator ability** translates to weighting tournaments or data sources of varying reliability (qualifier vs Grand Slam; futures vs ATP main draw).

### G. Genuinely open problems
17. **Diffusion models for skill or outcomes.** No published work.
18. **In-context Elo estimation by an LLM from a textual match history.** No published work.
19. **Neural Hawkes / point-process formulations for rating evolution.** No paper located applies these to player skill, despite matches being exactly an irregular event stream with intensity tied to activity and form.
20. **A foundation model for tennis, akin to RisingBALLER.** Masked-player or masked-match pre-training, BT head fine-tuned on win/loss. No obvious published competitor.
